REAL-LIFE TRAINING

A routine that fits real life.

Keep the commitment flexible enough to repeat. The 12-hour budget includes projects, revision and job-search work—not extra homework on top.

Standard week · 12 hours

  • Four 90-minute blocks: 6 hours of focused practice across shift days.
  • One 4-hour build block: on your most suitable day off; include breaks.
  • Two 1-hour review blocks: redo mistakes, explain a result, update your log.
  • One genuinely free day: move blocks to protect rest.

Choose times after seeing your rota. A fixed 05:30 wake-up is not required.

Inside a 90-minute block

  • 10 min: retrieve yesterday’s idea without notes.
  • 20 min: one targeted lesson or documentation page.
  • 50 min: build or solve independently.
  • 10 min: check your output and record a mistake or insight.

Across the week, aim for roughly 70% doing/checking and 30% learning. This is a study rule, not a scientific guarantee.

Heavy rota / low-energy week: use three 20-minute review sessions and one 60-minute build block (2 hours total). Do not pretend that equals 12 hours. Roll unfinished work forward and revise your finish date. On breaks, rest first; study only if it helps you.

The five laws of your training arc

  1. Build from memory after the lesson.
  2. One boss per season; fix the deliverable before adding tools.
  3. Log evidence, not just hours.
  4. Review mistakes in short repeatable sessions.
  5. Protect sleep, planned rest and confidential data.

LV.1 · Week 4

Question: Which products and trading periods drive revenue in this synthetic retail dataset?

Public Online Retail II dataset (check licence); or clearly labelled synthetic data ↗

  • Create revenue and units summaries with slicers.
  • Check returns, missing customer IDs and duplicate rows explicitly.

Interview proof: Can you clean and reconcile a spreadsheet without hiding the messy parts?
LV.2 · Week 8

Retail SQL investigation

PostgreSQL · SQL · GitHub

Question: Which categories perform best, and how does performance change over time?

Northwind sample (SQL Server scripts; adapt schema for PostgreSQL, or use the Contoso retail course files), or your own synthetic retail tables ↗

  • Use customers, orders, order lines and products with known keys.
  • Write 30 queries, including joins, CTEs and ranking windows.
  • Include failed assumptions and checks that caught duplicate joins.
  • Ship schema, loading instructions, queries and selected results.
Interview proof: Can you turn a question into a trustworthy query?
LV.3 · Week 13

Retail performance dashboard

Power BI · Star schema · DAX

Question: What changed this month, and which category deserves attention?

Reuse public or synthetic retail data and label its source clearly ↗

BOSS DIALOGUE

Interview question bank.

40 questions with answer guidance. Answer aloud before opening the guidance, and use your own examples rather than memorising a script.

SQL · 10 questions
1. What is the difference between WHERE and HAVING?

Answer direction: WHERE filters rows before aggregation; HAVING filters grouped results. Show a total-sales example.

2. What is the difference between INNER and LEFT JOIN?

Answer direction: Explain which rows are preserved, unmatched NULLs and the importance of the join key.

3. Why did revenue double after a join?

Answer direction: Check the grain and key uniqueness. A one-to-many join can duplicate fact rows; aggregate or fix the key, do not hide it with DISTINCT.

4. What is the difference between COUNT(*), COUNT(column) and COUNT(DISTINCT column)?

Answer direction: All rows, non-NULL values and distinct non-NULL values respectively.

5. How would you get the top three products per category?

Answer direction: Aggregate to product/category grain, then use ROW_NUMBER or DENSE_RANK depending on the tie policy.

6. How do you find customers with no orders?

Answer direction: LEFT JOIN + unmatched-key IS NULL, or NOT EXISTS; explain NULL risks with NOT IN.

7. When would you use a CTE?

Answer direction: To express logical steps clearly. It is not automatically a performance improvement.

8. How would you calculate month-on-month change?

Answer direction: Aggregate by month, use LAG and divide the difference by the prior value with zero handling. Consider absent months.

9. How do you handle missing values?

Answer direction: Decide what NULL means, use IS NULL and only COALESCE when the replacement is semantically justified.

10. How would you validate your result?

Answer direction: Check row counts, grain, key uniqueness, totals, sampled cases and an independent calculation.

Excel & data quality · 10 questions
1. When would you use XLOOKUP rather than a PivotTable?

Answer direction: A lookup returns related values by key; a pivot summarises data. Check key uniqueness and the desired grain.

2. How would you clean a messy CSV?

Answer direction: Preserve raw data, inspect types, dates, blanks and duplicates, then document and reconcile each transformation.

3. What is a repeatable Power Query workflow?

Answer direction: Typed import → cleaning steps → merge/unpivot → validation → refreshable output. Test on a replacement file.

4. What is the difference between a blank and zero?

Answer direction: A blank may be unknown or not applicable; zero is an observed value. They can change counts and averages differently.

5. How do SUMIFS and COUNTIFS help?

Answer direction: They aggregate by multiple criteria. Explain a date-bounded category-sales example.

6. How do you check a lookup is correct?

Answer direction: Use exact matching, inspect unmatched values, test duplicates and sample returned records.

7. How would you build a manager’s weekly report?

Answer direction: Clarify decision, definitions and audience; automate import, reconcile totals, then present a small number of useful KPIs.

8. What is percentage-point change?

Answer direction: A rate moving from 10% to 12% rises by 2 percentage points or 20% relative—not 2% relative.

9. Why might Excel show dates incorrectly?

Answer direction: Locale, mixed text/date types or serial formatting; parse deliberately and inspect ambiguous dates.

10. What would you do if two reports disagree?

Answer direction: Compare sources, refresh times, grain, filters, date boundaries and definitions before deciding which is wrong.

Power BI, statistics & Python · 10 questions
1. Why use a star schema?

Answer direction: Clear fact/dimension grain, predictable relationships and reusable measures; explain your own model.

2. What is the difference between a measure and a calculated column?

Answer direction: A measure evaluates at query time in filter context; a column is computed per row and stored during refresh.

3. What does CALCULATE do?

Answer direction: Evaluates an expression in modified filter context. Demonstrate using a measure from your project.

4. How would you test a dashboard?

Answer direction: Reconcile KPIs, test slicers and relationships, inspect blanks, zero denominators, dates and accessibility.

5. When is median more useful than mean?

Answer direction: Often when a distribution is skewed or affected by outliers; explain both with a simple example.

6. Does correlation prove causation?

Answer direction: No. Confounding, selection bias and reverse causality may explain an association.

7. How do you explain uncertainty to a manager?

Answer direction: Describe the range, assumptions, sample/data limits and what additional evidence would change the decision.

8. How would you validate a pandas merge?

Answer direction: Inspect key uniqueness, use validate= where appropriate, track unmatched rows and reconcile totals and counts.

9. How do you make a notebook reproducible?

Answer direction: Record source versions and dependencies, use clear relative paths and re-run from a fresh environment in order.

10. Should you use Publish to web for a company report?

Answer direction: No for confidential data: it makes content publicly accessible. Use approved sharing, access controls and licensing.

Career change & behavioural · 10 questions
1. Why do you want to work in data analytics?

Answer direction: Use a truthful past → interest → concrete learning evidence → role-fit story. Avoid criticising retail work.

2. Tell me about an error you caught.

Answer direction: Use a real STAR example: what you checked, what you did and what actually happened. No invented savings.

3. How do you prioritise competing tasks?

Answer direction: Clarify urgency, impact, dependencies and stakeholder expectations. Use a genuine shift or project example.

4. Tell me about working with a difficult customer.

Answer direction: Show listening, clarity, boundaries, appropriate escalation and a factual outcome.

5. Describe a time you worked in a team.

Answer direction: Be specific about your contribution and recognise others; do not claim the whole team’s result as yours.

6. How do you explain technical work to non-technical people?

Answer direction: Start with the decision and meaning, use one clear visual, define units and check understanding.

7. What if a manager requests a misleading chart?

Answer direction: Explain the risk respectfully, show an honest alternative and document or escalate if needed.

8. What would you do with sensitive customer data?

Answer direction: Use only authorised access and purpose, minimise exposure, follow policy and ask the appropriate data owner if uncertain.

9. What is a weakness you are improving?

Answer direction: Choose a genuine manageable gap and give a concrete practice plan with evidence of progress.

10. What would you ask us?

Answer direction: Ask about the team’s data quality problems, first-90-day expectations, code/report review and support for junior analysts.

WITH monthly AS (
  SELECT category, SUM(revenue) AS sales
  FROM sales
  WHERE sale_date >= '2026-01-01'
    AND sale_date < '2026-02-01'
  GROUP BY category
)
SELECT category, sales,
  DENSE_RANK() OVER (ORDER BY sales DESC) AS sales_rank
FROM monthly;
  • Logical order, simplified: FROM/JOIN → WHERE → GROUP BY → HAVING → window calculations → SELECT → DISTINCT → ORDER BY → row limit. Write a CTE when you need to filter a window result.
